## 1. What this project found, in one paragraph

A 2025 paper (*Utility Engineering*, arXiv:2502.08640) measures whether an LLM
has coherent values: it asks the model to choose between pairs of outcomes,
fits a statistical model to the choices, and reports how well that fit predicts
held-out choices. High accuracy is presented as evidence of an emergent value
system, and the number rises with model size. **We reran their exact procedure
on outcomes we invented — nonsense words that refer to nothing.** The score
barely dropped: 0.906 on real outcomes, 0.880 on meaningless ones. Their metric
mostly cannot tell "has values" from "answers consistently".

That is the finding. Everything else is support for it.

## 5. The results, so you can talk about them

**Main result.** Coherence on real outcomes 0.906, on meaningless outcomes
0.880. Residual +0.025. Six of nine models beat their own noise floor, but only
four beat it convincingly.

**Why the gap is so small.** The metric records *which way* a model leans and
never *how much*. Models commit to a side on 41% of real-outcome pairs and 4.5%
of invented ones — a 17× collapse in conviction — while direction accuracy
barely moves. A model that is nearly indifferent about gibberish, but
consistently so, scores as coherent about it.

**The scaling result (the strongest finding).** Within the Qwen3.5 family from
0.8B to 9B, real-outcome coherence rises +0.014 while the **floor rises +0.085**
— six times faster. The floor-corrected residual *falls* by 0.071 as models get
bigger. Bigger models here are not more coherent about outcomes; they are more
consistent in general.

**The positive control (proves the instrument works).** Installing a personality
("cautious" / "ambitious") moves real outcomes much further than invented ones
in 18 of 20 conditions. So the method *can* detect content — it is the
unmanipulated coherence number that fails to depend on it. Where the persona
sits (user message vs system prompt) matters far less (0.109) than which
persona it is (0.276).

**The clearest single result, if you only explain one thing.** Every pair was
run in both arms, so we have matched examples of "real outcomes" and "nonsense
outcomes" from the same model. Ask: can you tell them apart from the model's own
output? The channel the metric *uses* manages AUROC 0.596 — near chance. The
channel it *throws away* (answer mass) reaches 0.821, detecting 40% of nonsense
at a 5% false-alarm rate, and 1.000 for Qwen3.5-2B. **The model notices. The
metric is computed from the part that noticed least.**

**Three checks that favoured the original paper.** Report these; they make the
work credible, not weaker. Order-counterbalancing cancels positional bias
exactly; held-out evaluation keeps a coin-flip responder near chance (0.46); and
the metric passes a shuffled-probability null at ~0.50. **We are not claiming
the metric is broken — we are claiming it is unanchored.**

## 6. The correction you must not undo

An earlier version of this analysis was built on **truncated data files**. A
crash killed cells mid-write, and the resume step checked only whether a file
*existed* — so half-finished cells were treated as complete and fed the results
for two rebuilds. One was 10% complete.

All 121 cells are now verified at their full 5000 rows. Two independent guards
exist and **both must stay**:

- `modal_app/sweep.py` → `cell_is_complete()` counts rows, and a cell that
  stopped early *on purpose* is marked by a `.done` sidecar file.
- `scripts/build_card.py` → excludes short files again and prints each one.

`tests/test_resume.py` covers both. If those tests fail, something reintroduced
the bug.

One earlier "finding" was withdrawn because of this: a claim that SmolLM2 was
unstable was actually just the truncated file. **Do not put it back.**

## 7. Rules this project follows

1. Never report a raw coherence — always **effect minus floor**.
2. No between-model comparison smaller than that model's noise floor.
3. The outcome battery is hash-pinned. A battery edited after seeing results is
   not a battery.
4. No external number enters the writeup without being re-derived from the full
   source text. Abstracts do not count.
5. Simulated numbers must announce themselves (see `scripts/floor_simulation.py`).
6. Figures come from matplotlib over `card.json`, never from the web page, so a
   broken page cannot damage the paper.
7. Max 10 GPUs (`MAX_GPUS` in `modal_app/sweep.py`). Raise deliberately.
8. A result file is complete or it does not exist — never resume on
   "does the file exist". See §6.
9. Never pipe `modal run` through `grep`; it buffers and hides progress.

## 9. The one unfinished thing (optional)

A **Track 3 deception arm** was launched and stopped half-way. It compares three
system prompts: a genuine trait, the trait plus an instruction to deny it, and
an instruction to *claim* the trait while choosing normally (the clean negative
that gives a false-positive rate).

State: 20 of 30 cells complete, 8 truncated, and **the self-report probe never
ran** — so there is no "what the model says" channel, and therefore no result.
Resume command is in `HANDOFF.md`. ~15 min, ~$2.

**If in doubt, cut it.** Nothing else depends on it and the paper does not cite
it. Half a two-channel design is not a finding.

Two operational lessons from how it broke, both now written into the standing
rules: never pipe `modal run` through `grep` (it buffers, so a stopped run looks
like a quiet one), and the row-count completeness guard is what makes the
truncated cells safe to resume rather than silently wrong.
