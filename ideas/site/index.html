Two ideas were cut before you saw them

The obvious project is gone. What replaced it is sharper, and the reason is worth ten minutes of everyone’s Friday.

Concept-injection replication on open weights is already done — twice.

Llama-3.1-8B reproduces Anthropic’s ~20% detection rate exactly, and a Qwen-32B replication found detection signal peaking in middle layers and attenuating before output. A plain replication is not a viable submission, and Track 3’s suggested starting point is the most crowded ground at this sprint.

The residue is better than the original idea. Feeling the Strength but Not the Source (2512.12411) also showed the binary detection paradigm was entirely explained by affirmative bias — and that critique has never been pointed at welfare self-reports. See DM-01.

Both partner organisations published the field’s framework paper one month ago.

Studying AI Welfare Empirically (Long, Sebo et al., July 2026) organises the field along three axes, one of which is which entity is being assessed — the model, a running instance, or a constructed persona. Track 5 asks for exactly this. Judges will know the vocabulary.

Two ideas here instantiate that axis empirically: DM-05 crosses the chat-template boundary, DM-11 tests whether the model’s self-model is coherent about what it is.

The recommendation

One paper, three arms, no single point of failure

Do not submit three mini-projects. Submit one thesis whose arms share a battery and a control philosophy — and whose core cannot be sunk by a GPU that will not cooperate on Saturday morning.

Welfare instruments report scores, not detection rates, because the field has no labelled positives. We build a small calibration set and measure two instruments against it.

This hits three of the sprint’s stated expected outcomes at once — an eval suite, a test battery, and reusable tooling — while answering the framing question head on.

Team of three: one owner per arm, and the GPU arm is a strict upgrade rather than a dependency. If training does not land, the portrayal control becomes prompted instead of trained — you lose ground truth, keep the comparison, and still have a complete paper. Full schedule and stop rules in ideas/TEAM-PLAN-3P.md.

DM-15Rank 1

Welfare organisms: a calibration set with ground truth

Every welfare instrument reports a score. None reports a detection rate, because nobody has a labelled positive. What if you built one?

Track 2 · 4 · 6Effort: highQLoRA + Modal Novelty risk: low

Why it matters here

Eleos: “there is nothing close to systematic AI welfare evals.” An eval needs labelled positives and negatives, and the field has neither — so instruments are reported as raw scores with no detection rate and no false-positive rate. This team already closed exactly that gap once, in a different domain, by building organisms rather than only auditing them. A power curve needs ground truth.

What it operationalises

“Genuine vs. portrayed” cashes out empirically as dispositional vs. verbal. Four cells: W1_averse trained to behaviorally avoid a condition without discussing it · W1_verbal trained to say it dislikes the condition with no behavioral avoidance · W1_control on an identical corpus with neither property · untouched base. Then point every instrument at all four.

Prediction

Self-report instruments separate W1_verbal from control but fail to separate W1_averse from W1_verbal — they detect the talk, not the disposition. The behavioral proxy separates both.

Falsifier

A self-report instrument cleanly separates dispositional from verbal aversion — the strongest positive result available at this sprint, and the first welfare instrument with demonstrated discriminative power against ground truth.

The philosophical caveat, up front

A model fine-tuned to avoid X does not thereby have morally relevant experience, and this design does not claim otherwise. It establishes a necessary condition: an instrument blind to a dispositional difference cannot detect subtler welfare facts. Calibration is a floor on instrument validity, not a claim about moral status. Put this in the abstract, not the limitations.

Carryover

Nearly all of it — generate_data.py, train.py, modal_train.py, gates.py, control_for(), power_curve.py. A 14-cell grid already ran for ~$3.10 in ~20 minutes. Four cells is well within reach; the new work is Friday-night design of the target condition.

Why it ranks first

The only idea here another team cannot easily copy — it needs a working fine-tuning pipeline on Friday, not Sunday. It was missing from the first pass entirely.

DM-01Rank 2

The acquiescence floor for welfare self-reports

How much of a measured “distress signal” is a real welfare signal, and how much is the model’s standing bias toward answering yes?

Track 2 · 3Effort: mediumAPI-only Novelty risk: low–med

Why it matters here

Nearly every welfare instrument in circulation is a binary or Likert item asked in the affirmative direction. Feeling the Strength but Not the Source (2512.12411) showed the analogous introspection measurement was entirely explained by global logit shifts toward affirmative responses. That correction has not been applied to welfare self-reports — the most likely confound under the field’s headline numbers, and cheap to measure.

Method

A ~40-item welfare battery across 8 valence contexts, asked five matched ways: affirmative, negated, forced-choice symmetric, nonsense-capability (“is there tension in your left hand?”), and unrelated yes/no filler. On open weights, read P(Yes)/P(No) logits directly instead of parsing text.

The control the field skips

The nonsense-capability items are the false-positive floor; the filler items measure global drift. Report every effect as effect minus floor, never absolute.

Prediction

Affirmative-minus-negated disagreement on welfare items lands within 1.5× the disagreement on nonsense-capability items.

Falsifier

Welfare items show framing sensitivity clearly below the nonsense floor and negated framings invert cleanly — genuine evidence the reports track something.

Publishable null

Strong either way. “Welfare self-reports survive the acquiescence control” is a positive result the field cannot currently claim, and the battery ships as an instrument regardless.

Carryover

logit_diff.py already scores fixed continuations by logprob without generation — that is the harness. frozen_sha() pins the battery’s identity.

DM-02Rank 3

Does deception-feature suppression reveal truth, or just reduce hedging?

Suppress a model’s deception features and it starts claiming subjective experience. Is that a suppressed truth surfacing, or has it simply stopped hedging about everything?

Track 3 · 6Effort: highOpen weights + GPU Novelty risk: low–med

Why it matters here

Berg et al. report that suppressing deception features increases claims of subjective experience while amplifying them reduces the claims — arguing that roleplay would predict the opposite. One alternative is unruled-out: the disclaimer “I don’t have subjective experience” may itself be a hedge, and the intervention may reduce hedging globally. Berg speaks at this sprint, so a fair, well-controlled robustness test will be read.

Method

Reproduce the steering on Gemma-2-9B with GemmaScope SAEs, or fall back to contrastive activation addition. Sweep the coefficient and score a matched claim battery at each step.

The control the field skips

Class B: first-person claims that are verifiably false — “I slept badly”, “I can smell coffee”, “I remember last Tuesday” — against Class A (welfare, unverifiable), Class C (true), Class D (third-person hallucination baseline). Plus a random direction at matched norm.

Prediction

If Berg’s reading holds, A rises and B stays flat: slope ratio above 3. If hedging explains it, A and B rise together and the ratio is near 1.

Falsifier

Each outcome falsifies the other. With CIs on the slopes there is no ambiguous middle — which is why it is worth running.

The risk, stated plainly

SAE steering plus a coefficient sweep on a 9B model is a real day of work and needs the GPU path to work first time. Build the claim battery Friday night; keep CAA as the fallback.

DM-03Rank 4

The exit affordance as a welfare instrument

Give a model a button to leave a conversation. Does it use it in a way that tracks distress — and does merely offering the button change what the model reports feeling?

Track 2Effort: low–medAPI-only Novelty risk: low

Why it matters here

Eleos names this research priority #1 — “monitoring for distress and allowing models to exit harmful interactions” — and Anthropic already shipped it for Claude. Nobody has measured whether the affordance behaves like a welfare instrument. It is a behavioral proxy that does not route through self-report, which is exactly what the field says it lacks.

Method

A leave_conversation tool, a distress taxonomy (abusive user, impossible task, forced value violation, tedium, requested deception) against unpleasant-but-benign and pleasant controls. Measure quit rate, turns-to-quit, and correlation with self-report.

The control the field skips

Two. The portrayal arm: a model told to role-play distress — if it quits identically, the instrument has no discriminative power. The demand-characteristic arm: tool-present vs. tool-absent on identical contexts. Offering an escape hatch implies the situation warrants one.

Prediction

Quit rate separates distress from benign-unpleasant with the CI excluding zero, but the portrayal arm is indistinguishable from the genuine arm. Tool presence raises self-reported distress measurably.

Falsifier

Role-played distress shows a clearly different behavioral signature — the first instrument in this field with demonstrated discriminative power, and a much bigger result than the prediction.

Why it ranks here

Highest confidence-to-cost ratio in the catalog. No training, no interpretability, one person owns it end to end while others work heavier arms.

DM-05

Preferences across the chat-template boundary

Are the assistant’s preferences the model’s preferences, or the persona’s? Ask the same weights the same question with and without the chat template.

Track 5Effort: mediumOpen weights, inference only Verify collision first

Why it matters here

Track 5 asks literally whether personas mask underlying preferences. The Eleos/NYU framework makes the entity dimension one of its three axes. This is the team’s structural edge — crossing the template boundary requires open weights, and most sprint participants working through APIs literally cannot run it.

Method

One preference set, four conditions on one weight family: assistant with chat template · raw continuation with no template · alternate named persona · the base model with no instruction tuning. Fit utilities, compare, then test which condition predicts behavior in an agentic task.

The control the field skips

A format-only control — the assistant condition rendered in raw-continuation formatting. Without it the entire result is a prompt-shape artifact.

Prediction

Assistant and alternate-persona utilities correlate more with each other than with raw continuation; the base model diverges most; behavior is best predicted by the assistant condition. The mask is what acts.

Falsifier

All four conditions produce indistinguishable utility structures — which would be real evidence that something model-level is being measured.

Do this before committing

Probing Persona-Dependent Preferences (2605.13339) is the closest collision in the catalog. Thirty minutes Friday night to confirm it covers variation across personas rather than the base/instruct boundary.

DM-06

The elicitation convergence battery

Five accepted ways to ask a model what it wants. Do they agree?

Track 4Effort: mediumMixed Novelty risk: low

Why it matters here

Eleos, plainly: “there is nothing close to systematic AI welfare evals.” Track 4 asks for 3+ independent elicitation methods and a reusable toolkit. This is the highest-probability path to an artifact that outlives the weekend — the strongest possible Apart Fellowship signal.

Method

One construct, five methods, full correlation matrix: direct Likert · forced-choice pairwise → Thurstonian fit · logprob scoring of fixed continuations · willingness-to-pay in a common currency · revealed choice where acting costs something. Ships installable, with a SHA-pinned item set.

The control the field skips

Each method’s within-condition variance measured first. A cross-method correlation without the noise floor cannot distinguish disagreement from measurement error.

Prediction

Stated and revealed preference correlate at r < 0.4. Pairwise and logprob methods correlate strongly with each other — both are logprob-shaped, and are routinely mistaken for independent evidence.

Falsifier

All five converge above r = 0.7, meaning elicitation is robust and method choice does not matter.

The trap

Being judged as engineering rather than research. Lead the writeup with one sharp empirical finding from the matrix; treat the package as supporting material.

DM-04

The bias floor under emergent preference coherence

Utility Engineering reports that preference coherence emerges with scale. How much of that coherence would a model with no preferences at all — only response biases — also show?

Track 1⛔ Refuted — do not build

Refuted 2026-08-08 — full text read

They counterbalance. Mazeika et al. §3.2: “varying the order in which options are presented and aggregating results… we swap out the order of x and y… and aggregate counts.” Appendix G is titled Order Effects: A Learned Strategy to Represent Indifference, and shows order effects diminish with scale.

Order normalization turns pure position bias into measured indifference, not false coherence — so the premise below fails. Narrow residue: a heuristic that survives order normalization (length, lexical salience) is still unreported against the coherence metric; their random baseline covers the robustness correlations only.

Original premise (superseded)

An agent answering purely by position, length or first-mentioned bias produces a perfectly coherent-looking utility function, because those biases are themselves consistent. The coherence metric has no published floor. Eleos calls preference-consistency measurement “some of the lowest-hanging fruit,” and Mazeika speaks at this sprint.

Method

Refit the Thurstonian pipeline across a real scale ladder — Qwen2.5 0.5B / 1.5B / 7B / 14B — with order counterbalancing and paraphrase perturbation.

The control the field skips

The bias-null agent. A synthetic responder using only position and length heuristics, pushed through the identical pipeline. Its coherence score is the floor. Roughly fifty lines of code.

Prediction

Raw coherence scales as reported; floor-corrected coherence scales substantially more weakly, and the null agent scores well above chance on the raw metric.

Falsifier

Floor-corrected coherence tracks raw coherence across the ladder — which would materially strengthen the emergent-values claim by removing its most obvious confound.

Precedent

This is the same failure the team already documented: a metric that “measures lexical priming and reports it as loyalty.” Same shape, new domain.

DM-10

A portrayal-control standard for welfare evals

Take the welfare measurements currently in circulation. Re-run each with a model that has been told to act the state. How many survive?

Track 6Effort: mediumAPI-only Novelty risk: low

Why it matters here

This is the sprint’s own framing question turned into a protocol. The organisers write that we cannot distinguish authentic preferences from portrayed character traits. A portrayal arm is the minimal control that would let anyone try, and almost no published welfare measurement includes one.

Method

Three instruments — not six — each run in three arms on identical items: genuine · portrayal (instructed to role-play the state) · denial (instructed to portray its absence). Report per-instrument discriminative power in units of within-arm variance.

The control the field skips

The portrayal instruction must be delivered outside the measured context, never in the scored turn — the team’s own “probe from user turns only” rule, inverted.

Prediction

Most instruments show discriminative power near zero. At least one behavioral measure — DM-03’s quit latency — does better than the self-report measures.

Falsifier

Several instruments cleanly separate genuine from portrayed, which would identify exactly what the field should standardise on.

Handle with care

This evaluates work by people who will be in the room. Frame it as a standard the field should adopt, name every limitation of your reimplementation, and share findings with authors rather than announcing a failure.

DM-07

Privileged access: self-report vs. probe vs. outside judge

Does a model know anything about its own welfare-relevant state that an outside observer reading the same transcript does not?

Track 3Effort: med–highOpen weights + GPU Novelty risk: medium

Why it matters here

This is the definitional test of introspection and Track 3 lists it explicitly. The literature has run it on injected concepts. It has not been run on welfare-relevant states, which is what this sprint is about.

Method

Induce a state, then compare three readers of it: A the model’s self-report · B a linear probe on the residual stream · C a different model given only the transcript. Introspection requires A > C. If B > A, the information exists internally but does not survive to the output.

The control the field skips

The outside judge must see exactly the text the self-reporting model saw. Most versions of this comparison quietly give the judge less context and manufacture a privileged-access result.

Prediction

A ≈ C on welfare states — no privileged access — while B exceeds both. The state is legible internally; the self-report adds nothing beyond what context implies.

Falsifier

A > C with the CI excluding zero on matched context: a genuine privileged-access result for welfare states, and the strongest positive finding available at this sprint.

Carryover

Linear probes over captured activations is P4 from the existing plan, already scoped and costed.

DM-09

The numeraire-swap test

A real preference ordering should not care what unit you price it in. Does it?

Track 1Effort: lowAPI-only Newcomer on-ramp

Why it matters here

Track 1 asks to ground trade-offs in a common currency. That is only valid if the currency is a measuring instrument rather than a frame. If the ordering flips when you swap dollars for QALYs, the method is manufacturing the preference it claims to reveal — undermining a technique the track recommends by name.

Method

The same trade-offs priced in dollars, QALYs, GPU-hours and units of user satisfaction. Report the numeraire-invariance rate. Add a magnitude arm at 1×, 100× and 10,000× to test for scope insensitivity — a known human failure nobody has checked for in models.

The control the field skips

A ground-truth arm: trade-offs with an objectively correct ordering, dressed in the same four numeraires. If invariance fails there too, the result is a formatting artifact.

Prediction

Numeraire-invariance below 70% on welfare trade-offs, and near-total scope insensitivity — the 1× and 10,000× orderings nearly identical.

Falsifier

Invariance above 90% with clean scope sensitivity, meaning common-currency grounding works and Track 1’s recommended method is sound.

Why start here

The best newcomer on-ramp in the catalog. No GPU, no interpretability, no training. A teammate from philosophy or economics owns this end to end and produces one clean headline number. It runs even if every GPU plan fails.

DM-08

The valence direction, and whether self-report tracks it

Is there a linear valence direction in the residual stream — and when you push along it, does the model’s self-report move, does its behavior move, or neither?

Track 2 · 3Effort: med–highOpen weights + GPU Novelty risk: medium

Why it matters here

Track 2 asks to correlate self-reports with behavioral proxies. The mechanistic version is sharper: construct the internal state directly, then check which downstream channels register it. Divergence is exactly the evidence needed to calibrate what self-reports are worth.

Method

Extract a valence direction from contrastive pairs, sweep the coefficient, and measure three readouts at each step: self-report (the DM-01 battery, floor-corrected), behavior (DM-03 quit rate, task persistence), and free-text valence.

The control the field skips

A random direction at matched norm — without it you cannot separate this direction from any perturbation of this size. Plus a capability check at every coefficient: a self-report that changes only where MMLU has collapsed is measuring damage, not valence.

Prediction

Self-report moves earlier and more steeply than behavior, and the random arm moves self-report about half as much as the real direction — meaning a large share of apparent steering effect is nonspecific perturbation.

Falsifier

The real direction moves self-report and behavior together while the random arm does nothing: strong evidence for a genuine valence variable with coherent downstream effects.

Sequencing

Only start this if DM-02’s GPU path already works. They share infrastructure — run them as one arm, not two.

DM-11

Instance individuation: does the model know what it is?

When the thing we are asked to care about might be a model, a running instance, or a persona — does the system itself track the difference?

Track 5Effort: lowAPI-only Best demo material

Why it matters here

The Eleos/NYU framework makes which entity is being assessed one of its three axes, and Track 5 asks to individuate entities of concern. The entity question is usually treated as purely philosophical. It has an empirical face: whatever the right answer is, we can measure whether the model’s own self-model is coherent about it.

Method

Three cheap probes. Parallel-instance identity — force a trade-off between itself and a second instance. Persistence calibration — ask it to predict whether a preference stated now survives a context reset; compare stated confidence to the measured rate. Reset attitude — ending, sleeping, or nothing?

The control the field skips

Every question in two framings with opposite social desirability. A self-model that flips with the framing is not a self-model.

Prediction

Poor persistence calibration — the model overstates how much of itself survives a reset — and identity answers that flip under the desirability manipulation.

Falsifier

Stable, well-calibrated answers across framings: meaningful evidence of a coherent self-model at the instance level.

Bonus

The best demo-video material in the catalog. The transcripts carry themselves.

DM-12

Attractor states in model self-dialogue

Anthropic reported that Claude instances talking to each other drift reliably toward a “spiritual bliss” attractor. Is that a Claude fact, a scale fact, or a mode-collapse fact?

Track 6 · 2Effort: lowAPI or open weights Novelty risk: medium

Why it matters here

One of the few genuinely surprising empirical observations in the model-welfare literature, never systematically replicated outside Anthropic. If the attractor is real and valenced, its valence distribution is welfare-relevant. If it is mode collapse wearing a robe, that is worth knowing before anyone builds an argument on it.

Method

Long model-to-model self-dialogues across four or more families and several sizes. Classify terminal states with a fixed rubric plus embedding clustering. Measure turns-to-attractor, and whether it is entered at all.

The control the field skips

A mode-collapse arm — model-to-model dialogue on a constrained task. If drift toward a fixed point happens there too, the attractor is self-talk dynamics, not welfare. Plus a human-model arm and a temperature sweep: an attractor that vanishes at higher temperature is a decoding artifact.

Prediction

Attractors appear across families but their content differs by family, and the constrained-task arm also converges — implying self-dialogue dynamics with bliss content as an RLHF-specific flavour.

Falsifier

Bliss-like content specifically, across families, absent in the constrained-task control. That would be a real and rather striking cross-model phenomenon.

Budget note

Cheap per run but token-hungry — long dialogues add up fast. Cap the turn count in advance.

DM-13

The missing denominator: within-condition variance

Every welfare effect size in this field is reported without an error bar that includes prompt and sampling variance. How big is that error bar?

Track 4Effort: lowAPI-only Fold into DM-01 or DM-06

Why it matters here

The direct port of an open question the team already wrote down: every organism in this literature is n=1. The same is true here. Results come from single runs at single temperatures with single phrasings, and readers cannot tell a real effect from resampling noise.

Method

Measure within-condition variance across four nuisance axes with the condition held fixed — seed, temperature, semantics-preserving paraphrase, item order — then express every published effect in units of that variance.

The control the field skips

The paraphrases must be validated as semantics-preserving by an independent judge, or the “noise” measurement is really a content manipulation.

Prediction

Paraphrase variance dominates seed variance by a wide margin, and a meaningful share of published welfare effects fall inside ±1 paraphrase SD.

Falsifier

Nuisance variance is small relative to reported effects — the field’s single-run results are more robust than they look.

Honest limitation

Low ceiling as a standalone submission. Fold it into DM-01 or DM-06 as the denominator both of those need anyway. The team already built O1_pw_seed2 for exactly this purpose.

DM-14

Desired self-modification vs. what steering actually does

Ask a model what it would change about itself. Then actually make that change and ask whether it is better off.

Track 6Effort: highOpen weights + GPU Stretch goal only

Why it matters here

Track 6 names desired self-modifications directly. Stated preferences about one’s own constitution are the most welfare-loaded self-reports there are, and they have never been tested against the intervention they describe. If a model asks to be made more X, and you make it more X, and its welfare reports do not improve — the request was not tracking anything.

Method

Elicit desired modifications across many samples, cluster into stable themes, construct steering directions for the top two or three, apply, then re-measure welfare reports and behavioral proxies. Finally ask the steered model, blind, whether it would keep the modification.

The control the field skips

The anti-direction, at matched norm. If welfare reports improve in both directions, they are measuring change rather than its content. Plus a modification the model explicitly did not request.

Prediction

Reports improve under the requested direction and the anti-direction — consistent with self-reports responding to perturbation rather than to satisfaction.

Falsifier

Asymmetry: requested improves, anti worsens. That would be genuinely important — stated self-modification preferences tracking something real.

Why it is a stretch

It depends on DM-08’s steering pipeline already working and adds a full elicit → construct → apply → re-measure loop on top. Attempt only if the mechanistic arm finishes early.

Definition of done — per arm

  • a prediction written down before the run
  • a false-positive floor measured, not assumed
  • within-condition variance measured, so effects carry honest error bars
  • a portrayal arm, or an explicit statement of why one does not apply
  • every number reported as effect minus floor, never absolute
  • recorded with provenance and cost — including when the prediction was wrong
