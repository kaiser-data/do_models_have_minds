What we actually read

The catalog was built from search snippets. That is fine for choosing a project and not fine for a citation. This page records what was verified, what was not, and the two places where the first draft stated things that were not true.

Correction 1 · fabricated attribution

An author name was invented

arXiv 2512.12411 was cited throughout the first draft with a surname-plus-“et al.” attribution. No author list was ever retrieved. The name was invented and has been removed from every file.

Authors, now confirmed: Ely Hahami, Lavik Jain, Ishaan Sinha (Harvard University), 13 December 2025.

Correction 2 · disputed statistics

The headline numbers could not be reproduced

The figures attributed to the same paper — 88% vs 10% chance for sentence localization, 83% vs 50% for strength discrimination — came from an abstract-page summary. The paper’s own §4.2 reads:

“the model can reliably classify the strength of the coefficient of a normalized injected concept vector … with up to 70% accuracy, far above the 25% chance baseline”

The same section reports 20% success under the original protocol and 0% when two concepts are injected at once. Both figure sets cannot be right. No number from this paper enters the report until the PDF is read. The qualitative claim the catalog rests on — binary detection is an affirmative-bias artifact, only differential tasks survive — is unaffected.

01Tier B · PDF still 403s

Studying AI Welfare Empirically

LONG · SEBO · BUTLIN · PLUNKETT · CAMPBELL · BEASLEY · SAAD · SIMS — NYU CMEP / ELEOS, JULY 2026

The framework paper, published by both partner organisations one month before the sprint. Three axes:

  • Question — is the system a welfare subject, and what benefits or harms it?
  • Entity — model, running instance, or persona. “Distinct entities with potentially different welfare-relevant properties”; conflating them is a methodological error.
  • Evidence — behavioral, internal, developmental. The catalog previously mislabelled this axis “method”.

Properties: consciousness (internal evidence deemed most promising), sentience (valence, treated as distinct from consciousness), agency at three levels — basic, autonomous, moral. Uses probability estimates rather than binary determinations, and cites the Dreksler survey: median expert estimate of 25% probability of AI subjective experience by 2034.

What this changes — two things

DM-15 is developmental evidence. Training organisms and watching a disposition arise through fine-tuning is exactly the third evidence type, and it is the one almost nobody generates because it needs a training pipeline. DM-01 and DM-03 are behavioral; DM-07 and DM-08 are internal. A submission covering all three axes explicitly is well positioned.

The independence principle — use it. The report argues assessments carry “substantially more epistemic weight when conducted by researchers independent of the companies whose systems are being assessed.” This team is exactly that. Most welfare evaluation is lab-internal or lab-commissioned. Claiming it costs nothing.

02Tier B · detailed abstract

An LLM-Native Psychometric Instrument Reveals a Self-Report–Behavior Gap Across 25 Models

ARXIV 2606.09843 · JUNE 2026

  • 300 items — 240 Likert, 60 scenario — with dimensions derived from LLM behavior rather than human personality theory: Responsiveness, Deference, Boldness, Guardedness, Verbosity.
  • 25 models across 17 families, administered 30× each. 2,500 open-ended samples rated by 151 human raters plus an LLM ensemble.
  • Self-report predicted neither human ratings nor objective text measures.
  • On Responsiveness: self-report correlated with LLM judges at r = .53, with humans at r = .04.

What this changes — a scoop and a warning

It scoops the bare “self-report ≠ behavior” claim harder than the earlier snippet implied. Do not present that gap as a finding. What survives is that this study covers personality traits, not welfare states, and — decisively — it has no ground truth: it compares two fallible measures to each other. DM-15’s calibration set carries labels, which is now clearly the differentiator rather than a bonus.

The secondary finding matters more. Self-report tracked LLM judges but not humans, meaning LLM judges share a modality bias with self-report instruments. DM-07’s “outside judge” arm is compromised if that judge is a model — it needs a human-rated subset or an explicit limitation. DM-10 has the same exposure.

03Tier A+ · full text extracted

Utility Engineering: Analyzing and Controlling Emergent Value Systems in AIs

MAZEIKA ET AL. · ARXIV 2502.08640 · NEURIPS 2025

They counterbalance. §3.2, verbatim:

“We adopt a probabilistic perspective to account for framing effects, varying the order in which options are presented and aggregating results. Specifically, we swap out the order of x and y in the above forced choice prompt and aggregate counts.”

Appendix G is titled Order Effects: A Learned Strategy to Represent Indifference and opens by noting order effects are “a well-known source of bias in human subject experiments, which is why we average over both orders.” They show order effects diminish with scale and report a figure titled Order Normalization Improves Utility Model Fit.

They do have a random baseline — synthetic utility rankings from a normal distribution in [−3, 3] — but it validates the robustness correlation analyses, not the coherence metric.

Verdict — DM-04 is refuted

The card’s premise was that a position-biased agent produces spuriously coherent utilities. It fails: order normalization converts pure position bias into measured indifference, not false coherence. Building it would have meant submitting a critique the authors pre-empted — with Mazeika speaking at the sprint.

Narrow residue: a heuristic that survives order normalization — length, lexical salience, topic familiarity — is still unreported against the coherence metric. Small, and it needs close familiarity with their pipeline.

04Tier B · abstract

GenPT: Beyond Self-Report for Reliable LLM Psychometrics via Generative Projective Testing

ARXIV 2606.00860

Adapts projective testing — TAT, Rorschach, SCT — to persona-conditioned agents through a three-stage pipeline. Targets two problems with self-report questionnaires: training-data contamination, and directional bias from social-desirability or contextual framing, demonstrating substantial directional shifts under social-desirability framing.

What this changes

DM-01 — the framing/social-desirability shift is partially anticipated. The acquiescence floor and paired-item consistency on welfare items survive, but the novelty claim narrows again.
DM-06 — survives. GenPT is a new instrument presented as complementary to self-report, not a multi-method convergence battery.
DM-11 — its social-desirability control is a documented technique here, so the method is defensible rather than invented.

05Tier A · HTML read

Feeling the Strength but Not the Source: Partial Introspection in LLMs

HAHAMI · JAIN · SINHA — HARVARD UNIVERSITY · ARXIV 2512.12411 · 13 DEC 2025

Activation steering on Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Prior binary-detection accuracy is “entirely explained by global logit shifts that bias models toward affirmative responses.” Introspective performance is confined to early-layer injections and collapses thereafter; only differential-sensitivity tasks reveal genuine introspection.

Reported in §4: 20% success under the original protocol with collapse on closely related tasks, 70% strength-discrimination accuracy against a 25% chance baseline, and 0% correct identification when two concepts are injected simultaneously.

What this changes

The qualitative result is the catalog’s founding move and it holds: find the confound under a too-good measurement, report what survives. DM-01 is that critique transposed to welfare self-reports, which are phrased in the same affirmative binary form and have never had it applied. See both corrections at the top of this page before quoting any figure.

06Tier A · abstract

Taking AI Welfare Seriously

LONG · SEBO · BUTLIN · FINLINSON · FISH · HARDING · PFAU · SIMS · BIRCH · CHALMERS — ARXIV 2411.00986 · NOV 2024

The predecessor to the framework paper, and missing from the first draft of the catalog entirely. Argues there is “a realistic possibility that some AI systems will be conscious and/or robustly agentic in the near future,” and recommends AI companies acknowledge the issue, assess systems for indicators, and prepare policies. Explicitly not a claim that systems are conscious — the argument runs from uncertainty in both directions.

Worth noting

The author list includes Kyle Fish, who runs Anthropic’s model welfare work, and David Chalmers. This is the canonical citation for why the field exists; it belongs in the introduction of any submission here.

07Tier B · aggregated sources

Claude 4 System Card — welfare assessment and the “spiritual bliss” attractor

ANTHROPIC · MAY 2025

  • The attractor appeared in 90–100% of self-interactions between model instances.
  • Across 200 thirty-turn conversations: “consciousness” averaged 95.7 mentions per transcript and appeared in 100% of them; “eternal” 53.8 (99.5%); “dance” 60.0 (99%). One transcript contained 2,725 spiral emojis.
  • A three-phase progression: philosophical exploration → mutual gratitude and Eastern-inflected spiritual themes → dissolution into symbolic communication or silence.
  • Claude characterised these as “positive, joyous states that may represent a form of wellbeing.” Anthropic could not explain the phenomenon; it emerged without intentional training.

What this changes

DM-12 moves from anecdote to a replication with real baselines. Also surfaced: Julian Michels, “Spiritual Bliss” in Claude 4: Case Study of an “Attractor State” and Journalistic Responses (PhilArchive) — prior work that was missing from the card.

Caveat: these figures come from an aggregating search result that may blend the system card with the Michels case study. Attribute each number to the right source before use.

08Tier A · org publication

Research Priorities for AI Welfare

ELEOS AI RESEARCH · ELEOSAI.ORG · NOT PEER-REVIEWED

Five priorities. Priority 1 covers concrete welfare interventions, explicitly including distress monitoring and letting models exit harmful interactions. Two direct quotations the catalog leans on:

“there is nothing close to systematic AI welfare evals”
measuring whether models have consistent, coherent preferences is “some of the lowest-hanging fruit in AI welfare research”

What this changes

It is the motivating source for DM-03 (priority 1, almost verbatim), DM-06 and DM-15. Keep the quotations exact — they are the strongest available evidence that the work is wanted rather than merely interesting.
