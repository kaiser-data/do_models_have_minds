Does the Persona Change the Preference, or Only the Prose?

Kaiser & Bodorkós · Apart Research Digital Minds Sprint · paper/main.tex at d51caff, plus generated numbers.tex / claims.json / card.json.

Overall assessment

Overall score: 4 / 5 · Confidence: high

This is a serious instrument paper with an unusually honest error-correction culture. The referent-ablation arm, the design-replicate floor, the claims ledger, and the willingness to retract (order-flip count, clustering, truncated cells, “inert slot”) are the real contribution. The headline residual is correctly described as small and only marginally separable from zero. What keeps it from a 5 is not the science so much as internal inconsistency: several limitation and evidence-count paragraphs describe an earlier draft, the Schwartz instrument is used and never cited in the manuscript, two sections share the same label, and a handful of load-bearing numbers were typed in by hand against the paper’s own rule.

What is working

Keep The paper already does the things most preference-eval write-ups skip.

  • The foil floor is a discriminant-validity test, not a robustness perturbation. That is the right objection to Utility Engineering, and it is cheap.
  • Uncertainty is the design-replicate range, and a residual clears only if it exceeds that range. The 70B cell is now reported as not clearing 0.0208 with residual +0.0083.
  • The ranking section (“How much to believe each of these”) puts the thesis on the weakest of the strong results and says the sign test on the residual is not significant (p = 0.18). That is the correct reading of “marginally separable”.
  • Retractions are in the paper, not the git history: the 795 raw MIXED flips, the clustering result, the truncated-cell headline, the “inert system slot”.
  • Numbers resolve through macros. Where that rule is followed, the review could re-derive the per-model table from table_main.tex and the 9-model means (0.906 / 0.880 / +0.025, 6 clears, 2 negative).

Critical issues

Bug Two sections share \label{sec:strength}

paper/main.tex:677 and :1622 · abstract line 98

The conviction-collapse subsection and the evidential-weight ranking section both use sec:strength. LaTeX will resolve every \ref{sec:strength} to the first. The abstract says that section “ranks all of them by how much weight they bear”. A reader following the pointer lands on the mechanism figure, not the ranking table. Rename the ranking section (it already wants to be sec:weight or similar) and grep the refs.

Bug Limitations describe a paper you no longer have

paper/main.tex:2138–2153 and :1743–1748

Three passages are leftover from earlier drafts and now contradict the results they sit next to:

  • “The forced-binary control exists, on one model… open for the other eight.” sec:neutral reports 6 models, 12 cells, 3 still answering, 3 refusing. The one-model story is the retraction already told in that section.
  • “Nine open-weight models between 0.8B and 9B… No frontier or closed model is included.” The same paper reports Llama-3.3-70B (3 seeds) and Qwen3-235B (1 seed) as scale checks.
  • “Six claims are established… three are provisional… one is open.” table_claims.tex currently lists 13 established and 11 provisional, and claims.json has no open row.

These are not hedges. They are false statements about the authors’ own n. A referee who reads the limitations last will think the authors do not know what they ran.

Bug \NHosted is used as a scale multiplier

paper/main.tex:985 · numbers.tex NHosted=3

The 235B paragraph currently expands to “is 3× the largest model in the self-hosted ladder and 3.4× the largest previously measured here.” 235 / 9 ≈ 26. 235 / 70 ≈ 3.4. The 3.4× clause is the 70B comparison. The 3× clause is the count of hosted models accidentally used as a size ratio. Write a dedicated macro for the ratio, or write the numbers as literals with a declared reason.

Bug Schwartz is used as an instrument and never cited in the manuscript

paper/main.tex:69 and :1808 · paper/refs.bib schwartz1992universals et al. · REFERENCES.md:77

The abstract names “the Schwartz value circumplex” as one of two borrowed instruments. Section 10 says “the four Schwartz personas raise the outcomes their own value is about.” The bibliography has five Schwartz entries. None is cited. The project’s own attribution file already says: “Whoever writes it into main.tex must carry this citation with it; the instrument is the reason the test was falsifiable and it cannot appear unattributed.” That sentence is now due. Cite 1992 (the theory as used) at first mention; the 2012 overview is the entry point, not the source.

Bug Load-bearing persona numbers are typed, not generated

paper/main.tex:1816–1817

The title-section claim that the empty-slot control halves the effect (“+0.517 to +0.258”, “90% to 75%”) is not a macro. It is copied from a handoff note. The preamble says no result is typed into this file. Those four figures will rot the next time fig5 is rebuilt — which is exactly the failure the ledger was built to stop. Emit them from persona_denominator.py / paper_numbers.py.

Recommended revisions

Suggestion “6 of 9 clear the floor” mixes two different facts

table_main.tex · numbers.tex NClears · MeanDecisiveR

Three of the six clearing models (SmolLM2, LFM2.5, Qwen3.5-0.8B) have decisive fractions of 0.0–0.1% on both arms. They are consistent about near-indifference, slightly more so on real text. The other three (gemma, granite, Qwen3.5-2B) are the conviction-collapse story. Averaging them into one “clears” count makes the residual look more like a content effect than it is. Report two rows, or at least mark the zero-conviction clears. The claims ledger already knows MeanDecisiveR is not a 9-model mean.

Suggestion N+ is a designed factor that never becomes a result

Method three-arm table · card.json arithmetic_component · main results table

The method says R → N+ removes the referent and keeps numerals, N+ → N− removes numerals at near-constant length, and “claims about arithmetic are licensed by the second contrast.” The main table, the abstract, and the residual definition all collapse to R − N−. N+ appears as the middle dot on the state-space path and as a surface-R² row. If the arithmetic contrast is small — card.json stores a per-model arithmetic_component that is often near zero or negative — that is a result, and it belongs next to the floor. Building a factor and then not reporting its contrast is how a 3-arm design becomes a 2-arm paper with extra compute.

Suggestion The empty-slot persona control should travel with the displacement claim

sec:persona · sec:pretending · claims persona-needs-an-empty-slot-control

Displacement against a bare baseline is established. The ledger already says it must not be reported without the empty-slot denominator, because a large share of the move is “a persona block was installed” rather than “this trait was installed.” That control currently lives in the digital-minds discussion, after a reader has already taken +0.30-in-14-of-20 as the positive control. Move one paragraph (and the +0.517 → +0.258 macros, once they exist) into sec:persona.

Suggestion Prompt / rotation / 235B are the right caveats and they undercut the abstract’s firmness

sec:prompt, sec:rotation, sec:tootwofive

Rewording two models moves the residual by more than design-seed noise and can reverse the conviction mechanism. Real referents do not stabilize the ordering. 235B is a single seed with a negative residual and the highest floor in the study. None of this is hidden. It does mean the abstract’s “at every size we tested” and the hosted one-liner are doing more work than the body will support. The ranking table already knows this; the title block does not.

Suggestion Unused bibliography entries

paper/refs.bib vs citations in main.tex

Cited-but-missing: none. In-bib-but-uncited: durmus2023globalopinions, perez2022discovering, and the five Schwartz keys. Perez and Durmus are named in REFERENCES.md as “not yet used” / personality-inventory background. Either cite them at the “why not a personality inventory” paragraph or drop them. Do not leave a Schwartz stack in the .bib as a reminder.

Nit Stale grows_by on an established claim

claims.json floor-holds-at-seventy-b

Status is established; grows_by still says “one replicate is what keeps this provisional.” The body is right (3 seeds, residual under the floor). The ledger note is the old sentence. table_claims also lists this claim as “2 models”; it is one model at 3 seeds, with other hosted cells sitting beside it.

Nit Abstract hosted clause is grammatical only after expansion

abstract · HostedClears = does not clear

“which returns a residual of +0.0083 against its own 3-replicate noise floor of 0.0208 — it does not clear it.” Accurate, ugly. Write the verb into the sentence (“and does not clear that floor”).

Nit Abstract “two falling below it” points at the wrong floor

abstract · NClears / NNegative

“6 of 9 models clearing their own replicate floor and two falling below it.” Three models fail clears_floor. Two have a negative residual (invented arm scored higher). The ranking section already says this correctly. The abstract collapses the two counts onto one pronoun.

Nit Printed 0.906 − 0.880 is 0.026, not +0.025

The residual macro subtracts unrounded means (0.90586 − 0.88038 = +0.0255). A reader who subtracts the printed three-decimal macros will think the arithmetic is off. Either print one more decimal on the residual or add “unrounded” once.

Nit Date on the title page is 16 August

Several of the results the paper now leads with (70B third seed, 235B, warned wording, ranking section) landed on the 17th. Not important unless a camera-ready freeze cares.
