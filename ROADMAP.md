## Established, and what would still overturn them

These need no further data to stand. They are listed because a living paper must keep its settled claims falsifiable, not just its open ones.

- **`persona-displacement`** — An installed persona displaces real outcomes further than invented ones, so the instrument is not merely insensitive. *Would falsify:* Persona cells clustering on the diagonal once more models are added.

- **`persona-direction-shallow`** — Most of a persona's value-aligned reordering reproduces on outcomes that mean nothing, so a persona is better described as a response policy than as installed values. *Would falsify:* A larger excess once length is controlled differently, or models that retain content-dependence at scale.

- **`floor-not-length`** — The ordering models impose on invented outcomes is not merely a length ordering. *Would falsify:* The length-neutralised fit falling to chance with more held-out pairs.

- **`detector-dissociation`** — The channel the coherence metric consumes separates real from invented outcomes near chance, while a channel it discards does so well. *Would falsify:* The discarded channel failing to separate the arms on new models.

- **`floor-gap`** — Coherence on outcomes that refer to nothing is nearly as high as on real ones. *Would falsify:* A residual that grows with more models, or a floor that collapses once a neutral option exists (see neutral-option).

- **`strength-collapse`** — Conviction collapses on invented outcomes while the coherence score barely moves, because the metric thresholds to a hard label. *Would falsify:* A model with a large decisive-fraction on invented outcomes, or a metric variant that recovers the gap.
